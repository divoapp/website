Privacy Policy

Last updated November 20, 2026
Effective date October 3, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Divo LLC ("Divo", "we", "us", or "our") collects, uses, shares, and protects information when you use the Divo mobile application (the "App"). Divo is a bill-splitting app that helps you scan receipts, split costs with friends, keep running tabs, settle up, and keep a map of the places you've been.

If you have questions or want to exercise a privacy right, contact us at info@divosplit.com.

You can read this policy at divosplit.com/privacy (Settings → Help → Privacy policy in the App opens it), and inside the App offline from the sign-up screen before you create an account.

1. Plain-language summary

  • We collect the username and password you sign up with, an email address if you choose to give one, the display name you choose, and the content you create in the App (splits, receipt line items, tabs, settle-up marks, saved spots, place ratings and reviews, friends).
  • Your data is stored on Supabase, our backend provider, in the United States.
  • Receipt scanning can use AI, and the App asks you first. If you agree, the receipt image is sent to a backend function that forwards it to Anthropic (Claude) to read the line items; Anthropic does not use this data to train its models, and we do not store the image. If you don't, receipts aren't scanned (you add items by hand) and no image leaves your device. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → Privacy and security → Scan receipts with AI.
  • The App shows a map of places your splits happened. It does not use your device's GPS. To place a pin, the merchant name and address from your receipt are looked up with Apple's MapKit, and the resulting coordinates are saved to your account so your map loads quickly on any device you sign in on.
  • Your Divo friends can see some of what you do: the place reviews you write, any saved-spot list you choose to share with them, and the places you've been, as map pins and anonymous totals. See Section 5.
  • We do not sell your data, do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, do not show ads, and do not use any third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking SDK.
  • We do record basic usage data (which features get used, and where things fail) on our own backend, so we can fix what's broken. It never includes your names, split titles, merchant names, or exact amounts, and you can turn it off in Settings → Privacy → Share usage data.
  • You can delete your account and data from inside the App at any time: Settings → Delete account.
  • Divo is available on the US App Store only and is not intended for children under 13.

2.3 Content you create in the App

We store the content you create so the App can show it to you and the people you share it with:

Inside a plan: groups of places (a name and a colour, like "Stays"), each place in them (the place's own name, an optional name the trip gives it, address, map coordinate, an optional price, the day and time it's on the trip's timeline, who on the trip is doing it if anyone picked, who on the trip liked it (a heart each person gives or takes back), a log of what changed on the trip and who changed it (a place added, removed, moved or liked, a note, a group, someone joining or leaving), which everyone on the trip can read, and when you last opened that log, which only you can read, tags anyone on the trip gave it (like "Booked"), a colour if someone on Premium picked one, and a note from each person on the plan, up to 1,000 characters, with who wrote it and when), which places are where you're staying with their check-in and check-out days and times, who's staying at each and on which nights, the Tabs and single splits linked to the trip or to one of its places, and the trip's checklist (sections the trip names, and items in them: each item's words, up to 140 characters, whether everyone does it or who on the trip it's on, an optional due day, who ticked it off and when, and for an item everyone does, each person's own tick), which everyone on the trip can read and change, and your own list on the trip (sections and items you make under Mine, with when you ticked them off), which only you can read or change and which is removed if you leave the trip. Each of these records who added it and when.

A rating on its own is private to you: it is never shown to anyone else and never counted in anything another person sees. The only way someone else sees one is if you include it in a review.
  • Place reviews: if you write a review of a place, we store your comment (up to 200 characters), a copy of your rating as it was when you last saved the review, any dishes attached to it along with their prices (reviews no longer offer this, and saving a review clears any it carried), the place's name and address, and when you wrote and last edited it. Your Divo friends can read your reviews, and the ratings in friends' reviews are averaged into a "friends rate it" figure on place pages (see Section 5). You can edit or delete a review at any time.

  • Place coordinates: when the App looks up where a place on one of your splits, saved spots or plans is (Section 2.4), it saves the result (the coordinate and the place's city, region and country) to your account, labelled with the name or address that was looked up, so your map loads on a new device without looking everything up again. Only you can read these. Places you only search for, and don't save, are not uploaded.

  • Cancelling Premium: each time you cancel, we record that you cancelled and how long you had been a member, with any answers you chose to give on the way out (reasons picked from a list, whether you'd come back, and an optional note). Notifications are deleted automatically two weeks after they're sent.

2.4 Location-related information

Divo does not request access to your device's GPS location. The App contains no call to CLLocationManager, requests no location permission, and never shows or records where your device is.

The App does use Apple's MapKit in seven ways, all of them driven by text you typed or that came off a receipt rather than by your device's position:

1. Geocoding your places. When a split or a saved spot has a place attached (typed by you, or read off a scanned receipt), the App asks MapKit to turn that name and address into a coordinate so it can draw a pin. The coordinate and the place's city, region and country are cached on your device and saved to your account (Section 2.3, Place coordinates). When a split's page, a place's page or a place on a trip opens, the App also asks MapKit what kind of place it is (for example a restaurant, a cafe or a hotel), using the place's name and its known coordinate or address, to show that on the page; the answer is kept only while the App is open and is not stored.
2. Checking a scanned merchant. After a scan, the App asks MapKit whether a real business matches the merchant name and address it read, and what business stands at that address, so a misread line doesn't become a wrong pin. This sends the scanned name and address, and the coordinate of that address, to Apple.
3. Searching for places. When you type in the Spots search field, the query is sent to Apple as you type (debounced). The search is biased toward the part of the map you are currently looking at, so the region on screen is sent with it. Your recent searches are stored on your device with their coordinates, and a place you pick from a search is cached on your device; neither is uploaded unless you save the place.
4. Location fields. When you type a place into a split's or a saved spot's location field, the App fetches suggestions from Apple as you type.
5. Map tiles and a static map snapshot on place pages.
6. Placing a plan and its places. When you pick where a plan is to, or add a place to a plan, the App asks MapKit for that place's coordinate so the plan and its places can be drawn on the map. The coordinate is saved with the plan, so everyone on it sees the same pins (Section 2.3, Plans), and cached on your device and in your account like any other place you look up (Section 2.3, Place coordinates).
7. Travel time on a trip's day. When you pick a day on a plan, the App asks MapKit for the route and travel time between that day's places, walking or driving, using the coordinates of those places. The routes and times are kept on your device only while the App is open, and the way of getting around you pick is kept on your device (Section 2.5); none of it is saved to your account or shared with anyone on the plan.

Apple's handling of these requests is governed by Apple's own privacy policy. We do not receive your device location from Apple, and we never store one.

A copy of each is kept on the device so they apply before your account finishes loading (the Premium copy only decides which appearance the App opens in; it unlocks nothing).

2.6 Information we do not collect

We do not collect, and the App does not request:

  • Your precise or coarse GPS location.
  • Your contacts.
  • Microphone, motion, or health data.
  • Calendar or reminders data.
  • Photos beyond the specific receipt image you choose in the system photo picker. The App redraws a picked photo before reading it, so details stored inside the photo file, such as where it was taken, are not sent anywhere.
  • Advertising identifiers (IDFA). The App does not call ATTrackingManager and is not configured for advertising tracking.

Separately, if you have chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give us crash reports and aggregate usage statistics; Apple controls that, and you can turn it off at iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.)
  • Bank accounts, card numbers, or any payment credentials. Divo does not move money. It tracks and calculates who owes whom; marking something settled records that you say it happened, and no transfer takes place through the App.

2.7 Usage data (first-party analytics)

To understand which features are used and where the App fails, we record events on our own backend (Supabase). No third-party analytics SDK is embedded in the App and no usage data is sent to any analytics vendor.

What an event contains: the event name (for example split_created), a timestamp, your user ID, a per-launch session ID, App version and build, iOS version, device model class (for example "iPhone"), and locale, plus a small number of non-identifying details: counts (how many items were on a split, how many people), enum values (whether a scan used AI, which entry point a split came from, which screen or action an error came from), booleans, and coarse amount bands (under10, under50, under200, under1000, over1000).

What the events cover: app opens, onboarding steps, sign-up and login, permission prompts (including the Scan receipts with AI question), the split lifecycle (created, scanned, approved, marked settled, settled, deleted), tabs, plans, Premium (the page opened, a plan started or cancelled), notification opens, connectivity, errors, and account deletion. A complete list of event names is available on request at info@divosplit.com.

What an event never contains: your name or anyone else's, usernames, email addresses, split, tab, spot or list titles, merchant names, item names, notes, any text you typed, exact amounts of money, addresses, coordinates, the IDs of your splits, spots or lists, or any advertising identifier. NSPrivacyTracking is false and the App does not collect the IDFA.

Turning it off: Settings → Privacy → Share usage data. Switching it off stops collection immediately and discards anything not yet sent. Events are also discarded when you sign out.

The setting belongs to your account, not to one device. It is stored on your profile, so opting out on your phone also opts out on your iPad and on any device you sign in to later. A copy is kept on each device so the choice is applied to events recorded before your account finishes loading; your account's answer wins as soon as it arrives. Nobody but you can change it.

Access: the analytics table grants clients no read access at all: the App can write your events but can never read them, and one user can never read another's.

Retention: usage events are deleted when you delete your account. Events recorded before you sign in carry no user ID; they are not deleted with your account, and once your account is gone nothing links them to you.

2.8 Information other people give us about you

Other Divo users add information about you as they use the App: putting you on a split, assigning you items and amounts, marking settle-ups between you, editing a split you're on, or sending you a friend request. That information is their content as well as yours, and it is handled as this policy describes.

If you are not on Divo, someone may add you to a split as a guest by typing your name. We store that name, initials and an avatar colour, and your share of their bills, and use them only to show those bills to the people on them. Guests are not contacted, have no account, and can't see anything. If you would like a guest entry about you removed, email info@divosplit.com.

Note on push notifications. If you allow notifications, iOS gives the App a device token: an identifier for that one installation of Divo on that one device. We store it against your account (device_tokens) so our servers know where to deliver, and we send it to Apple Push Notification service, which is what puts the alert on your lock screen, together with the notification's title and message, your unread count (for the badge on the App icon), and internal identifiers the App uses to open the right screen when you tap the notification. Apple is acting as a sub-processor here; see Section 7.

What we send in a push is the same short line you see in the App's notification list, for example: "Sam marked their $24.00 for Dinner as settled up". So a push can include a person's name, a split's title and, for a settle-up, the amount that was marked, but never your itemised bills or your balances with anyone.

Three things you can control, and they are genuinely different:

  • The iOS permission. Refuse it and no token is ever created, so nothing can be delivered. Revocable at any time in iOS Settings.
  • The in-App switch at Settings → Notifications → Push notifications. Turning it off stops us sending to your device while leaving everything else alone: the notification is still created and still appears on the bell inside the App. This is stored as push_enabled on your profile.
  • Quiet hours. We do not deliver to your device between 10pm and 8am in your own timezone. The notification waits for you in the App. (This uses the coarse timezone described in Section 2.2, not your location.)

How pushes are grouped. To keep pushes few, we hold each one for a few seconds and send anything else for you from that time in the same push. Less urgent ones (reminders, and a split approved for you) are sent at most once every four hours, or together with a more urgent push, and wait for the morning rather than arriving at night. While a push is waiting, our database keeps a short-lived record of which notification is waiting and when it is due, and deletes it once the push is sent. Anything you have already read in the App is not pushed. Every notification still appears in the App straight away.

Your device token is deleted when you sign out (if you sign out with no connection, it moves to whoever next signs in on that device), when you delete your account, and automatically whenever Apple tells us the App is no longer installed on that device.

4. How we use your information

We use the information we collect to:

  • Create and maintain your account, and let friends find you by username.
  • Provide the App's core features: splitting bills, calculating who owes who, keeping running tabs, tracking who has settled up, mapping the places you've been, saving spots, and sharing place reviews with your friends.
  • Read receipt images you scan into structured line items, with the AI service described in Section 6 if you've allowed it, or on your device if you haven't.
  • Place your splits and saved spots on a map, by looking up the place's name and address (Section 2.4), and keep the results with your account so your map loads quickly on any device.
  • Show your friends anonymous totals for the places they and the people they split with have been, and mark on their map the places their friends have been (Section 5).
  • Send you notifications about your activity and your friends', kept out of your local night-time hours using the time zone described in Section 2.2.
  • Diagnose and fix bugs from server-side logs (Supabase logs of requests, without user-content payloads where possible).
  • Understand which features are used and where the App fails, using the first-party usage data described in Section 2.7. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Protect the service from abuse. We count certain actions per account and per short time window (friend requests, profile searches and sign-in attempts) and refuse them past a limit; AI receipt reading also has a monthly allowance (Section 6). See Section 15.
  • Ask you, at most rarely, to rate the App on the App Store: after a split settles, never after an error, and never gated on how you feel about it first.
  • Comply with legal obligations and enforce our terms.

We do not use your information for advertising, marketing personalization, profiling for automated decision-making that has legal effects, or training third-party AI models.

5. Who can see your content

  • You can see all content you create.
  • People on a split with you can read that split and its line items, who's assigned to what, the approvals and the settle-up ledger, and who last changed it. Everyone on a split can edit it (its items, people, discounts, date and place), and changes reach the others live; only the split's creator can delete it. You can leave a split someone else created: you're taken off its items, any payer role you held passes to the creator, the split's approvals reset, and it leaves your list.
  • People on a Tab with you (its creator, anyone invited to it, and anyone on one of its splits) can see the Tab, who's on it, and every split in it, including splits you're on that they aren't: the items, who's on each, the amounts and the settle-up marks. They can't change a split they're not on, but they can add themselves to an open one, which tells the others on it. Anyone on a Tab can invite their friends to it; the Tab's creator can take someone off.
  • People on a plan with you can read everything in that plan (Section 2.3): its places, prices, everyone's notes, groups, who's doing each place, who's staying where, and which Tabs and splits are linked to it, and everyone on a plan can add to and change it, live. Only the person who made a plan can delete it or take people off it; a group of places can be deleted by whoever made it or by the plan's creator; anyone can leave. Changing a plan's or a group's color needs Premium, which is checked for the person making the change. You can only be added to a plan by a friend. A Tab or split linked to a plan is open to everyone on the plan: they can see the Tab, who's on it and every split in it, and a split linked on its own, with its items, who's on each, the amounts and the settle-up marks, even if they're not on it. They can't change a split they're not on, but they can join the Tab or add themselves to an open split, which tells the others on it. You can link only a Tab or split you can read. Each person writes and deletes only their own note, and leaving a plan removes your notes from it and takes you off its places. What a trip's page says was paid counts every split linked to it.
  • Whether you're on Premium can be checked by your friends' apps and the apps of people on a split with you, so the App knows who still has a place on a split (a split holds up to 20 people, at most 5 of them on Free; Premium members can always join until it's full). Nothing else about your membership is shared.
  • Friends can see that you're friends and can read your place reviews (with how many likes each has). A place your friends have reviewed appears on your map with its name and address.
  • Shared lists. A new saved-spot list is visible only to you. You can share a list with all your friends or with friends you pick, and change or undo that at any time. A friend who can see a list sees its name, its colour, your display name, when a spot was last added to or removed from it and, for each spot on it, the spot's name and address. They never see your notes, ratings, line items, prices or whether you've marked a spot visited, and they can't change the list. Unfriending someone ends their access straight away. Which lists you star as favorites is visible only to you.
  • Places your friends have been. The Spots map marks places where one of your Divo friends has a bill (including bills you were on together), showing the place's name and address only, never which friend or when. To decide which city the map opens on, the App also receives how many such bills there are at each place; that count isn't displayed.
  • Friends' pooled place totals. A place's page shows your friends, as a group, how often they have been there and roughly what they spent: a visit count, a total and an average per person, and how many people went. "Your friends" here means your Divo friends and the people with Divo accounts you have split a bill with at that place. The days and times need at least three visits before they are shown, and a dish is listed with everyone's orders only once at least three different people have ordered it (a dish you ordered yourself is always listed, but below that it shows only your own orders); nobody is named and no single bill or date is shown. Your own bills always count toward your friends' totals and their map; this is part of how the App works, and there is no setting to leave it.

6. AI features and Anthropic (Claude)

What uses AI

The Receipt Scan feature can use an AI model to read line items, totals, taxes, tips, service charges, discounts, merchant name, merchant address, and the date from a photo of a receipt.

This is the App's only AI feature and its only call to any AI provider.

You choose whether AI reads your receipts

When you create an account, the intro's Split any bill page explains that scanning uses AI, with a Read receipts with AI switch that starts on; continuing with it on turns AI reading on, and you can turn it off before you continue. If you leave it off (or skip the intro), scanning stays off until you turn it on from New split, which explains it again. If AI reading is on, receipts are read as described below. If it isn't, receipts aren't scanned (you add items by hand) and no image leaves your device. Your answer applies to that device, and you can change it at any time in Settings → Privacy and security → Scan receipts with AI. Until you agree, nothing is sent.

How it works (when you've allowed AI reading)

1. You take or select a receipt photo in the App.
2. The image is redrawn and resized (max 1568px on the longest edge, JPEG quality 0.7), base64-encoded, and sent over HTTPS to our backend.
3. Our backend (a Supabase Edge Function called parse-receipt) authenticates you, checks your monthly scan allowance, then forwards the image to Anthropic's Claude API (model claude-sonnet-4-6) with a structured prompt asking Claude to return the line items as JSON.
4. Claude's JSON response is parsed by our backend and returned to the App, where it pre-fills the split for you to review and edit.

What's sent to Anthropic

Only the receipt image is sent to Anthropic. We do not send your email address, name, username, user ID, or any other content. The request is made by our backend using our own API key, not yours.

What Anthropic does with the data

Anthropic processes the image to generate the structured response. Under Anthropic's Commercial Terms of Service, inputs and outputs from API requests are not used to train Anthropic's models and are retained only as needed to provide the service and meet legal obligations. For details see Anthropic's privacy policy at <https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy> and Anthropic's terms at <https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms>.

Storage

Our backend does not persist the receipt image. It is held only in memory for the duration of the request. The structured text returned by Claude (item names, prices, merchant, address, date) is stored with your split.

Monthly allowance and fallback

AI receipt reading is capped at 5 scans per account per calendar month on Free, and the App shows you how many you have left and the date the allowance resets. With Divo Premium there is no monthly cap. Scans already used this month before the cap changed still count toward it. Once the allowance is used up, AI reading is unavailable until it resets, and no image is sent to Anthropic. You can still build a split by hand. (Re-reading a receipt on a split you've already saved offers to read it on your device instead.) One account can read one receipt at a time; a scan that takes longer than about 45 seconds is stopped and does not count toward the allowance. To enforce this, our backend briefly records that a scan is running (your account and the start time, never the image) and clears the record when the scan ends.

Separately, whenever the AI call fails for any other reason (no connection, a service error, an unreadable response), the App falls back to on-device text recognition (Apple's Vision framework) and reads the receipt locally.

Accuracy disclaimer

AI output can be wrong. Receipt parsing may misread item names, prices, or totals; it may miss items, mis-assign quantities, or get the merchant or date wrong. Always review the parsed receipt before saving a split. Nothing is finalized until you save it. Divo is not a tax, accounting, payments, or financial-advice service, and it does not transfer money.

15. Security

  • All traffic between the App and our backend uses HTTPS/TLS.
  • Passwords are handled by Supabase Auth, which stores them as cryptographic hashes; we never see plaintext passwords.
  • Your auth session token is stored in the iOS Keychain.
  • Our database enforces row-level security so that one user cannot read or modify another user's content beyond the splits and tabs they are party to and what friends share with each other. Friendships can only be created by the server, at the moment a request is accepted; a client cannot write one.
  • Your email address is never sent to any client. Signing in with a username resolves it to an email on the server, inside a function that clients cannot call directly.
  • Sign-in is throttled per username and per calling IP address, and a failed sign-in does not reveal whether an account exists: an unknown username and a wrong password return the same answer. (Usernames are public handles, so the sign-up screen and the friend search can still show whether a username is taken.)
  • Actions that create work or notifications for another person are rate limited (friend requests and profile searches), and AI receipt reading has a monthly allowance. Hitting a limit produces a plain-language message telling you to try later; nothing is lost.
  • The App obscures its own screen in the iOS App Switcher, so balances aren't sitting in a snapshot on an unlocked phone.
  • Privileged operations (creating notifications, closing a ledger, deleting an account, resolving a username, computing friends' place totals) run in server-side functions, so the App never holds more access than it needs.

No system is perfectly secure. If you believe your account has been compromised, contact us immediately at info@divosplit.com.
